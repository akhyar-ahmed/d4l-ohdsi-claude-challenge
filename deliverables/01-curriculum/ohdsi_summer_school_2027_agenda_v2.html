Use case thread: Anticoagulation monitoring in elderly patients across German hospital sites
Clinical question: Do elderly patients (≥75) discharged on oral anticoagulants (DOACs / VKAs) differ in their follow-up monitoring rates across three German hospital contexts — and can we detect this from FHIR-exported EHR data standardised to OMOP?

This question is deliberately chosen because it hits the two unavoidable German data obstacles on every day of the week: PZN drug coding (DOACs are prescribed by package, not ingredient — rivaroxaban 20 mg film-coated tablet, PZN 09295940, is not the same OMOP concept as rivaroxaban) and FHIR-to-OMOP mapping (MII KDS Medication profile → DRUG_EXPOSURE is non-trivial). Neither is optional. Both are lab problems, not lecture slides.

OMOP vocabulary & the PZN problem
How OMOP vocabulary works: source codes → standard concept IDs. The standard drug hierarchy: ingredient → clinical drug → branded drug → drug package. PZN (Pharmazentralnummer) as a package-level identifier: no native OMOP standard concept. The ATC bridge: PZN → ATC code → RxNorm/RxNorm Extension via OMOP vocabulary chains. Where the mapping is lossy and why that matters clinically (rivaroxaban 10 mg vs 20 mg — different indication, same ingredient). Usagi walkthrough for concept mapping. PHOEBE for concept set recommendations.

Federated research without data transfer — EHDEN, DARWIN EU, and German governance
Why data stays local: MII Broad Consent, 16 Landeskrankenhausgesetze, GDPR. The federated model: queries run locally, only aggregated results leave the site. Building the anticoagulation cohort in D2E; analysis in Python/R; drafting the network study protocol
Core cohort definition: patients ≥75 discharged with an ICD-10-GM I48 (atrial fibrillation) or I26 (pulmonary embolism) diagnosis AND a DOAC or VKA DRUG_EXPOSURE within 30 days of discharge AND at least one INR or anti-Xa MEASUREMENT within 90 days.
