Why this week exists

Landing-page copy

Most health data cannot answer the question you want to ask it. It is coded differently in every hospital, it sits in silos, and it cannot legally leave the building. The OMOP Common Data Model and the OHDSI toolchain were built to solve exactly that — and OHDSI Germany, founded in 2021, now has roughly 130 community members and around 8 data partners working on it.

This summer school takes you through the whole chain once, on one clinical question, in five days: from raw FHIR bundles, through vocabulary mapping and ETL, through data quality and characterisation, to a cohort definition and a federated network-study specification. You work in a group of five on a German-flavoured use case — anticoagulation monitoring in elderly patients across three synthetic hospital datasets — and you present your result on Friday.

The platform is Data2Evidence (D2E): the open-source, license-free OMOP platform released by Data4Life in April 2025. It bundles the OHDSI tools you will use all week — White Rabbit and Rabbit-in-a-Hat for scanning and ETL design, DQD for data quality, Achilles for characterisation, PHOEBE for concept sets, ATLAS for cohort definitions — with Prefect for pipeline orchestration and a Jupyter gateway for Python and R. It is federated by design: analyses run where the data is, and only aggregated results leave the site.

The point of the week
You leave with D2E running on data you control. Friday afternoon is a supported installation lab on your own laptop or your institution's server — not a demo.

Who runs it

Data4Life — a nonprofit healthtech organisation supported by the Hasso Plattner Foundation and co-lead of the OHDSI Germany national node together with TU Dresden and Medical University Lausitz / Carl Thiem Cottbus — hosts the school. It does not run the programme alone: at least half of the teaching faculty are external to Data4Life, drawn from the OHDSI, EHDEN and MII/NUM-DIZ communities. Faculty list to be announced. TBC

Teaching language is English.

Who should apply

Eligibility

We have 30 places. They are meant for people who can put OMOP to work at their own institution after the week — not only for people who find it interesting.

You are eligible if all of the following apply

  • Career stage: PhD student, early-career researcher (roughly up to 8 years post-degree TBC), or clinician with a research role.
  • Affiliation: a university, university hospital, research institute, public health body, registry, or a non-profit healthcare organisation. Europe-wide; applicants from outside Europe are welcome but travel is at their own cost.
  • Data: you work with — or have a realistic path to working with — patient-level health data at your institution. This can be EHR, claims, registry or cohort data. It does not have to be in OMOP yet; that is the point of the week.
  • Technical baseline: you are comfortable with SQL and with either R or Python, and you can install Docker on the machine you will bring.
  • Commitment: you can attend all five days. This is a single continuous use case; partial attendance does not work.

What you need to bring

  • A laptop running macOS or Ubuntu on which you have administrator rights, with Docker and npm installable. D2E is deployed as containers; a detailed pre-arrival install guide is sent with the acceptance letter.
  • Optional but strongly encouraged: access to a dataset of your own that you may legitimately use during the week. Bringing your own data is what turns the Friday lab into a working installation rather than an exercise.
You do not need to bring identifiable patient data to Potsdam
The teaching use case runs on synthetic data. If you bring your own data, you keep it under your own institution's governance and on your own infrastructure — the federated model means nothing has to be transferred to us, and we do not ask for it.

What you submit

Application package — all fields in the form below
  1. A short CV (max 2 pages, PDF): education, current position, relevant methods and publications.
  2. Your data statement (max 250 words): which dataset you work with or expect to work with, roughly how many patients, what it contains, who at your institution authorises access, and whether any part of it is already in OMOP.
  3. Your research question (max 200 words): one observational question you would want to answer with this data. Characterisation, effect estimation or prediction — say which.
  4. An adoption statement (max 200 words): what would change at your institution if you came back with OMOP and D2E working. Who else would use it.
  5. A letter of support (1 page, PDF, signed by your data steward, DIZ lead, department head or supervisor): confirms that you may spend the week on this and that the institution supports an OMOP evaluation. TBC — whether this is mandatory or optional
  6. Technical self-assessment: SQL, R, Python, Docker, prior OMOP/OHDSI exposure.

Everything is submitted in English through the form below. Incomplete applications are not scored.

How we select

Selection criteria and scoring — published in advance, applied by a human committee

The purpose of this school is adoption of an open-source OMOP toolchain in European research institutions. The criteria are weighted accordingly: two thirds of the score is about whether OMOP will actually be used at your site after the week.

CriterionWeightWhat scores well
1. Data access core 35% A named, existing dataset the applicant can realistically work with, with a named person or body that authorises access. A concrete path to access scores higher than a large dataset the applicant cannot touch. MII/NUM-DIZ sites, registries, hospital EHR, claims and cohort studies all qualify.
2. Institutional weight core 25% Evidence that the applicant can move their institution: a signed letter of support, a role with a mandate (DIZ staff, data steward, methods group lead, PI), colleagues who would use the result. One trained person inside a data-integration centre is worth more to the network than five interested individuals without a site.
3. Technical readiness 15% SQL plus R or Python at working level; ability to run Docker on the machine they bring. Enough to keep up with five afternoon labs. Prior OMOP experience is a plus, not a requirement.
4. Research question & motivation 15% A specific, answerable observational question rather than a topic; a clear reason why standardisation is the obstacle; realistic expectations of what OMOP does and does not solve.
5. Network value & balance 10% Applied at cohort level after scoring: spread across countries and institutions, a mix of clinicians and methodologists, and representation of sites not yet in the OHDSI Germany network. Maximum 3 participants per institution. TBC

Process

  • Each criterion is scored 0–4 by two independent human reviewers; scores are weighted and summed. Divergences greater than 2 points are resolved by a third reviewer.
  • The selection committee is composed of D4L organisers and external faculty. TBC — names
  • Reviewers declare conflicts of interest and do not score applicants from their own institution or their own group.
  • No automated or AI-based scoring is used. See the data protection note below.
  • A waiting list is kept until TBC in case of withdrawals.
  • Decisions are communicated by e-mail with a short reason. Feedback beyond that is not provided.

Data protection note

Required because this form collects CVs, research topics and letters of support — all personal data under the GDPR
No AI scoring. No automated decision-making.
Application data is not entered into any LLM, AI assistant or automated scoring agent — not for ranking, not for summarising, not for pre-filtering. All scoring is done by named human reviewers reading the applications directly. There is therefore no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. Should the organisers ever want to change this, it would require a separate legal basis, a data protection impact assessment, an update to this note, and a documented human-in-the-loop with a right to contest the decision — it cannot be introduced quietly.
